    How the ramp is built. The seed is converted to OKLCH. Lightness is distributed evenly from L+spread down to L−spread, clamped to 0.05–0.95 so no step collapses into pure black or white. For every step the maximum chroma that still fits inside sRGB is found by binary search on the gamut boundary, then multiplied by your chroma percentage — which is why the lightest and darkest steps are naturally less colourful. Nothing here can produce a Display-P3 colour: a value only leaves sRGB when its chroma exceeds that boundary, and the boundary is exactly what every step is clamped to. The badge in the header re-verifies each generated step and would turn red if any escaped.

    Why the advisor doesn’t use a fixed “optimal chroma” band. Tools that warn when chroma leaves a set range — say 5–25% — are hue-blind, and the sRGB boundary is not. At hue 207° nothing above C 0.146 exists at any lightness, so a 25% ceiling is unreachable advice; at hue 330° the gamut runs to C 0.317, so the same ceiling is needlessly strict. Worse, the band scales badly at the extremes: at L 0.10, hue 207°, the real ceiling is C 0.017 — a colour at C 0.30 is not slightly over, it is roughly seventeen times over. Everything the advisor reports is measured against the boundary for your actual lightness and hue.
